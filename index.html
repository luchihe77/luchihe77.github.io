About

My research sits at the intersection of strategic human capital, organizational design, and organizational theory. I study how organizational design choices shape employees' performance and career advancement, especially during two transitional phases of work life: when employees first join a firm and when they later go through significant organizational changes such as restructuring or mergers and acquisitions.

During these periods, employees adapt to new environments and experience cognitive “unfreezing,” making them especially receptive to external influences. My work explores how incentive systems, task assignments, promotion structures, and the integration of algorithms can influence employees’ role perceptions, motivation, skill development trajectories, performance, and long-term career success.

Methodologically, I primarily use quantitative analysis with econometric techniques, complemented by field observations and interviews, across hospitality, retail, and technology settings.

Current Research

Joining a New Firm

Research on how early organizational design choices influence employee adaptation, motivation, and long-term career outcomes when individuals first enter a firm.

Organizational Change

Research on how restructuring, merger and acquisition, and other organizational transitions reshape employee behavior, performance, and skill development.

Incentives and Task Design

Research on how incentive systems, task assignments, and promotion structures alter employees’ perceptions of roles and their career trajectories.

Algorithms at Work

Research on the integration of algorithms in organizations and how algorithmic decision-making changes work experience and outcomes.
